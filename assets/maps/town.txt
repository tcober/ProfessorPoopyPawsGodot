; Alembic Town — 80x56 tiles (1280x896 px), Basil's origin village: a forest-floor
; clearing with FOUR GREAT TREES standing along its north edge, each carrying a
; round RING DECK near its crown, a door in its trunk, and a rope ladder down.
; Tiled by assets/_gen_tileset_town.py on the shared OverWorld driver;
; collision/markers by scene/alembic_town.gd.
;
; THERE IS NO ELEVATION SYSTEM AND THERE DOES NOT NEED TO BE ONE. A cell is exactly
; one (x, y) with one legend char and one walk/solid bit, so storeys are unambiguous
; IFF THEY ARE DISJOINT IN THE GRID. `stratum:` names the storey; TileScene's
; assert_strata is the whole system, and every failure in it is SILENT.
;
; THE CANOPY IS NOT A FLOOR. It is four ISLANDS. The previous build made both canopy
; storeys continuous deck from edge to edge, and a town that is 100% floor renders as
; horizontal stripes of plank and fascia — a lumber yard, or a fence. What makes both
; references (FFXIV's Slitherbough, Endor) read is the VOID between the platforms.
; So: each ring is a disc of decking with solid leaf all round it, and the town —
; the lane, the shops, the well, the stall, the cottages — is on the FOREST FLOOR,
; which is the part that already read as somewhere.
;
; THE TREE BLOCK is 12 cols x 9 rows, and ITS WALKABLE CELLS ARE NOT AUTHORED BY HAND.
; They are the rasterization of the very ellipse tree_ring draws — `_tree_props.ring_cells`
; makes the mask, `_alembic.assert_all` checks this grid against it, and a cell is
; walkable iff its CENTRE is on decking. Do not "tidy" these rows: they are derived.
;
; The build before this one guessed them, and guessed a tight blob INSIDE the disc. At
; the east and west poles two whole columns of drawn decking were solid and the south
; rim was two cells too generous, so a round platform was walked as a RECTANGLE with an
; invisible wall following its rim the whole way round — and the widest rows of the disc
; were the NARROWEST rows you could walk, which is the inverse of a circle. Nothing
; complained: a cell that looks like deck and stops you looks exactly like a cell you
; have not reached yet.
; THE MASK IS THE BODY'S BOX, NOT A POINT, because the overhang is DIRECTIONAL and no
; single radius can express it. (row 4, col 2) sits at o = 0.65 — comfortably inside
; anything you would call the rim — and a body standing there has its west foot off the
; platform, because its feet are ten pixels south of its centre and the arc has already
; curved away. (row 1, col 1) sits at o = 0.88, far worse by radius, and is fine, because
; on the top half of an ellipse those same ten pixels point further IN. So the 12x8
; collision box is placed at each cell centre and all four corners must clear the RIM
; BOARD (o <= 0.90) — not the outer edge: the rim is the deck's finished lip, the
; handrail stands just outside it, and walking to the edge put a body at the poles
; standing ON the rim with the rail between its ankles.
;   row 0      leaf bed               solid         — the disc IS drawn here, and it is
;                                                     under the crown's 96-100% opaque
;                                                     heart. A body there is invisible;
;                                                     _check_art's walk-behind lint says
;                                                     so in numbers. Exception 1.
;   row 1      the north arc          WALK canopy   — you pass BEHIND the tree here
;   row 2-3    the shaft (cols 4-7)   solid         — the DOOR is drawn on row 3; these
;                                                     are the widest walkable rows,
;                                                     which is the widest part of the
;                                                     ellipse: a circle, not a rectangle
;   row 4      the south deck         WALK canopy   — 6 cells, the row under the door
;   row 5      the south rim          solid, EXCEPT the ladder's two columns. The arc is
;                                                     at y=92, so a body centred on this
;                                                     row has its feet past it wherever
;                                                     you put it. The LANDING is
;                                                     exception 2 and it is named off the
;                                                     ladder, not typed as a row: it is
;                                                     the one place you are meant to
;                                                     stand on the lip, because standing
;                                                     there IS stepping onto the rungs
;   row 6      the fascia crescent    solid         — the ladder head cuts through it.
;                                                     THERE ARE NO BAND CELLS HERE. The
;                                                     ring's fascia is a crescent painted
;                                                     inside one sprite, not a run of
;                                                     face cells, so it gets no `v` and
;                                                     no mask_band. It had both, and the
;                                                     mask shipped as a 16x12 SLAB OF
;                                                     DECK PLANKING drawn over anything
;                                                     standing on the row above — a body
;                                                     cropped to the head, one either
;                                                     side of the ladder. A terrace's
;                                                     walkable row ENDS at its face, so
;                                                     its crest must come back over your
;                                                     sunk feet; the ring's deck stops a
;                                                     WHOLE ROW north of the arc, so a
;                                                     body pressed south there overhangs
;                                                     onto more deck and there is nothing
;                                                     to swallow
;   row 7-8    the trunk's flanks     `j`           — greattrunk, NOT `y`. Typed `y` the
;                                                     flanks render leaf against the
;                                                     ladder's plank class and the
;                                                     autotile draws a pale transition
;                                                     wedge either side of the rungs,
;                                                     which reads as a clipping bug
;                                                     because it is one
;
; THE SHAFT GOES DOWN FIRST AND THE RING GOES DOWN ON TOP OF IT. Order is the whole
; difference between a trunk that FEEDS THROUGH the platform and one CHOPPED OFF under
; it. Blitted last, the shaft's own `edge()` top — dead straight, 64px wide — lands two
; px under the crescent and the tree visibly BEGINS at a horizontal cut: a post nailed
; to a saucer. Blitted first from block row 6, that straight edge is buried under opaque
; decking and the only line crossing the trunk is the fascia's hem, which is an ARC — and
; an arc crossing a cylinder is an overlap. `_alembic._shade_under` then lays the deck's
; own shadow on the bark for the 20px below that hem, because bark under a platform and
; bark in the open are otherwise the same six colours.
; THE RAIL IS NOT DECKING AND YOU DO NOT WALK ON IT. Its near arc is a Tier-3 prop
; (`RingRail`) keyed at the block's south edge, so every body on the ring draws BEHIND
; the handline; the far arc stays baked with the deck, because it is behind you.
; `R` is the walkable RIM twin — same terrain as `y` (ringedge, which renders LEAF), so
; the part of a rim cell the disc's curve does not cover shows canopy rather than plank
; fabric. Give those cells a deck underlay and the leftover corners square the circle
; straight back off.
; The ladder (cols 5-6) runs down INSIDE the trunk's 4 columns, so it has solid timber
; on both sides for its whole length — otherwise you could step sideways off it onto
; ground that is meant to be thirty feet below. It runs THROUGH the foot to the floor,
; because a link whose last cell butts into solid timber joins the ring to nothing.
;
; THE FEET SIT AT THE CLEARING'S NORTH EDGE. A trunk is 4 solid columns; a tree with
; its foot halfway down the map cuts the floor into strips you must walk around. The
; first draft did that with four of them and the clearing came out as five corridors.
;
; THE SCHOOL IS ITS OWN SCENE. What stays here is the way there — the north lane at
; cols 35-36 — and the `school` anchor sits in its mouth, so town_thesis's dash ends
; at a place you walk into rather than at a building that is no longer in this grid.
;
; THE THREE SHOPS ARE 5x4, THE SAME ENVELOPE AS A COTTAGE, and that is deliberate. They
; were `tree_hut`s at 3x4 — a cone of thatch over a barrel of withy staves, which is the
; right building for a BOUGH and a tiki hut on a forest floor: one tan value throughout,
; no wall material, and three cells wide next to a five-cell cottage. `_town_props.town_shop`
; is the town's own envelope (leaf canopy, greeny cement, copper, vines) plus an awning, a
; lit display window and a hanging device. Roof rows are `x`/`p`/`i`, body rows `X`/`P`/`I`,
; the `D` centred on the bottom row; the anchors sit one row SOUTH of it.
